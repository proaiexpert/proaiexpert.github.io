03 / BEFORE CONTACT

The problem begins before the first call.

Accounting clients often arrive with uncertainty already in motion: they may not know which service fits, which records matter, whether a notice needs attention or what can be shared safely. Financial Stream needed a client path that could reduce that uncertainty in two languages before a calendar link ever appeared.

Situation patterns

  • A notice without a clear next step
  • Incomplete or unreconciled records
  • Needs spanning bookkeeping, Payroll and tax
  • Documents that require context before review
  • A preference for English or Russian

These are situation patterns reflected in the service and intake architecture; they are not direct client quotations.

04 / SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Five layers. One clearer path.

Each layer answers a different client question. Together they move a visitor from first impression to an informed next step without asking automation to replace professional judgment.

Verified project scope · Live EN/RU website architecture · July 2026

  1. 01

    Trust and presentation

    A calm bilingual opening establishes who Financial Stream serves, what the business handles and how to begin.

  2. 02

    Services and client self-selection

    Services are grouped around recognizable situations so visitors can identify the closest path without already knowing accounting terminology.

  3. 03

    Structured intake

    Complex or document-heavy matters begin with context. A shorter message remains available when the request is simple.

  4. 04

    Content and search

    Service pages and practical Materials give visitors useful preparation while creating distinct, indexable entry points.

  5. 05

    AI and automation

    Chat and workflow support handle defined tasks with capability-specific status and visible human boundaries.

Capability status is disclosed individually in Chapter 9; the five-layer map does not imply that every automation is fully operational.

05 / BILINGUAL EXPERIENCE

Two languages. One service logic.

The English and Russian experiences follow the same decision path: explain the service, set expectations and offer a clear next step. The structure stays aligned while each language is allowed to read naturally.

06 / SERVICE ARCHITECTURE

Services organized around real situations.

The architecture groups related work into three families, making it easier to recognize the closest starting point even when the final scope still needs clarification.

01

Business setup and documents

Business formation, company setup, initial accounting context, financial consulting and document review.

02

Bookkeeping and Payroll

QuickBooks bookkeeping, cleanup and catch-up work, reconciliations, Payroll records and L&I reporting.

03

Tax and reporting

Business and individual tax-return preparation, Sales Tax, Washington DOR reporting and filing-related documents.

07 / INTAKE BEFORE BOOKING

Context before calendar.

A calendar is useful only after the request has enough shape. The intake sequence helps Financial Stream understand the situation, identify missing context and choose the next practical step.

  1. Structured requestComplex or document-heavy matters begin with guided context.
  2. Context reviewFinancial Stream reviews the situation before proposing a conversation or service path.
  3. Document and access clarificationThe client is told what may be needed and what should not be sent through an unsecured message.
  4. Calendar or practical next stepBooking follows when a conversation is useful; otherwise the next action can be clarified directly.

Do not send full Social Security numbers, passwords, login credentials or sensitive documents through the short-message form.

08 / CONTENT AND SEARCH

Content designed to guide and create distinct search entry points.

Localized service and article pages do more than translate information. Each page creates a distinct search entry point shaped around the language, terminology, real questions, service intent, geography and audience it is meant to serve.

A visitor may search for an accounting service, a Seattle, Federal Way or Washington location, or help in a preferred language—and reach a page written for that combination of intent. The same architecture can support Russian, Ukrainian, Japanese or another language that matters to a service business, without treating localization as a mechanical copy of the English site.
