## Final verdict

The pair is strategically aligned but independently authored.

Both articles help a U.S. service business evaluate a website provider and written proposal before signing. They share the same evidence and risk-governance foundation, but they use different entry points, organizing frameworks, examples, and editorial language.

No mechanical translation or paragraph-by-paragraph mirroring is required.

## Shared primary intent

Help the reader determine whether a proposed website engagement clearly defines:

- the business objective;
- scope and exclusions;
- responsibility and client inputs;
- third-party dependencies;
- rights, access, billing, and transfer;
- acceptance evidence;
- document traceability;
- changes and post-launch support.

## Shared factual and legal alignment

PASS:

- Proposal, SOW, contract/agreement, estimate/quote are distinguished without interpreting a specific document.
- Important sales promises should be written into the SOW, contract, or clearly incorporated attachment before signing.
- Scope, responsibility, client input, dependency, acceptance, and traceability are separate dimensions.
- Third-party dependency does not replace project ownership or responsibility.
- Payment does not automatically transfer every copyright interest.
- Initial copyright ownership and later transfer are separate.
- Transfer of copyright ownership generally requires a signed written document, except for transfer by operation of law.
- Work made for hire is limited.
- Domain registrant, registrar account, DNS, billing, and access are separate controls.
- Accessibility evaluation should not rely on one automated tool.
- The articles do not interpret a specific contract or provide legal advice.

## Deliberate non-parity

Approved differences:

- RU uses the risk-of-rework / paying-twice framing; EN uses procurement and risk reconciliation.
- RU provides more explanation of U.S. terminology for a Russian-speaking reader.
- EN uses named tools such as Proposal Risk Ledger and Definition of Done.
- RU includes a seven-area overview; EN moves directly from reconciliation into the Ledger.
- Section order and examples differ.
- The two CTAs use equivalent intent but natural language for each audience.
- Word count and paragraph count do not need to match.
