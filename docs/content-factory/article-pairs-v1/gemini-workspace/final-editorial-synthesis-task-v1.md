# 6. FINAL EDITORIAL PRINCIPLES

Each final article must satisfy all of the following.

## Authority through usefulness

Authority must come from:

- accurate definitions;
- practical decision systems;
- explicit boundaries;
- concrete examples;
- source traceability;
- careful distinction between fact, risk, recommendation, and hypothesis.

Do not manufacture authority through:

- excessive jargon;
- named frameworks that add no meaning;
- unnecessary English labels inside Russian copy;
- exaggerated warnings;
- unsupported market claims;
- artificial executive tone;
- repeated references to E-E-A-T.

## Premiumity through restraint

Premium quality must come from:

- strong opening logic;
- disciplined hierarchy;
- clean transitions;
- specific language;
- controlled density;
- memorable but truthful framing;
- tools that help the reader make a decision.

Do not use hype, fear, dramatic absolutes, or generic luxury language.

## Information first

The article must fully answer the searcher's question before asking for contact.

The CTA should feel like the natural next step for a reader whose situation is complex, not a sales interruption.

## Independent localization

RU and EN versions must be strategically aligned but independently written.

They must not be mechanical translations or paragraph-by-paragraph mirrors.

RU must sound natural to a Russian-speaking business owner operating in the United States.

EN must sound like native, professional U.S. business editorial writing.

Facts, obligations, service boundaries, and core conclusions must remain consistent across the pair.
