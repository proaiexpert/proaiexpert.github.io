## 1. Strategic alignment

PASS.

Both articles help a U.S. service-business buyer evaluate a written website proposal before signing.

Shared decision dimensions:

- reconcile proposal, SOW, contract/agreement, estimate/quote, and attachments;
- normalize scope before comparing price;
- distinguish the seven proposal-review areas;
- evaluate each material item through separate Ledger fields;
- separate responsibility, client input, and third-party dependency;
- distinguish legal rights from practical control;
- define acceptance evidence before launch;
- separate revisions, change requests, defects, and maintenance;
- evaluate evidence rather than portfolio appearance alone;
- use risk labels only after the complete review;
- resolve, exclude, or knowingly accept red risks;
- preserve the boundary between operational review and legal advice.

## 4. Proposal-document boundary

PASS.

Both versions distinguish:

- proposal;
- SOW;
- contract/agreement;
- estimate/quote;
- attachments.

Both advise the reader to move important sales promises into traceable written documents before signing.

Neither version concludes that an oral statement is automatically invalid or legally unenforceable. Both state that legal effect depends on the actual text and applicable law.

## 5. Proposal Risk Ledger parity

PASS.

Both articles preserve the complete multidimensional model:

- item or required result;
- scope;
- responsible party;
- client input;
- external dependency;
- acceptance evidence;
- document source;
- open question.

Both preserve:

- explicit-exclusion logic;
- `NOT APPLICABLE` / `НЕ ПРИМЕНЯЕТСЯ` where a field does not apply;
- the principle that one item can simultaneously be included, provider-assigned, client-dependent, third-party-dependent, and fully defined.

Neither uses a single status to replace the model.

## 6. Seven-area map versus Ledger

PASS.

Both articles clearly preserve the seven substantive proposal areas:

1. business objective;
2. scope;
3. content and responsibility;
4. technology and dependencies;
5. rights and practical control;
6. acceptance;
7. post-launch support.

They also preserve the separate Ledger fields. The two systems are not collapsed into each other.

## 7. Copyright and domain accuracy

PASS.

Shared copyright facts:

- payment does not automatically transfer every copyright interest;
- initial ownership and later transfer are separate;
- qualifying work made for hire is limited to circumstances established by law;
- transfer of copyright ownership, other than by operation of law, generally requires a signed written document;
- third-party licenses remain separate;
- source files and repository access must be addressed independently.

Shared domain/control facts:

- registrant and registrar account are distinct concepts;
- DNS control, billing, renewal notices, administrative access, and transfer path must be checked separately.

Both use U.S. Copyright Office and ICANN sources adjacent to the claims. Neither says the registrant is the universal “true owner” or that a provider-controlled domain is automatically “rented.”

## 8. Accessibility boundary

PASS.

Both versions state that:

- accessibility evaluation should begin early and continue through development;
- no automated tool alone can determine whether a site is accessible;
- knowledgeable human evaluation is required;
- one Lighthouse score is not a complete definition of quality.

W3C is not framed as a universal legal-compliance conclusion for every private business.

## 9. Acceptance and change-control consistency

PASS.

Both versions distinguish:

- launch from acceptance;
- revision from change request;
- defect from maintenance.

Both require method/evidence, pass condition, and launch-blocking status for important checks.

Neither presents acceptance as a universal legal conclusion.

## 10. Evidence discipline

PASS.

Both versions distinguish:

- live website;
- screenshot;
- Concept Project;
- template;
- redesign mockup;
- case study;
- testimonial;
- verified metric.

Both state that a screenshot demonstrates presentation, not process or business performance.

Neither adds a client outcome, ranking, lead, conversion, revenue, or ROI claim.

## 11. Risk-label and decision-gate parity

PASS.

Both apply green/yellow/red only after reviewing:

- scope;
- responsibility;
- client input;
- external dependencies;
- acceptance evidence;
- document source.

Both pair color with an explicit text label.

For a red risk, both provide the same three choices:

1. clarify and document;
2. explicitly exclude;
3. knowingly accept the consequences.

Neither uses a universal weighted vendor score.
