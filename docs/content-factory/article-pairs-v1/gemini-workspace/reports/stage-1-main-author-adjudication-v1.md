## PARTIALLY ACCEPT

### 1. Stronger editorial voice

Accept the need to reduce dry consulting language, but do not replace factual precision with provocative marketing language.

Desired tone:

- more decisive;
- more human;
- more concrete;
- still calm and evidence-based.

### 2. Local examples

Accept adding U.S. service-business scenarios.

Use clearly hypothetical or composite scenarios unless a real client example is approved and documented.

Washington/Seattle references should appear only where they improve relevance naturally. Do not keyword-stuff geography.

### 3. Table transformation

Accept that the final rendered pages should not present a long sequence of visually identical tables.

Do not automatically replace every semantic table with interactive cards.

Each data structure must be evaluated by function:

- preserve a table when row/column comparison is essential;
- use cards for independent scenarios;
- use a step rail for sequence;
- use accordions only when collapsed content does not hide critical comparison;
- preserve no-JS access and semantic reading order.

### 4. CTA concepts

Accept proposal-review and multilingual-architecture CTAs in principle.

Final CTA wording must match actual approved services and intake capacity. Do not claim legal review or impartiality beyond the actual service model.

## REJECT OR DO NOT ADOPT AS WRITTEN

### 1. Proceed directly to Stage 3

Reject.

The report itself did not provide enough verifiable search evidence to authorize implementation.

### 2. "Technical base is perfect / ideal"

Reject as too absolute.

The current technical guidance is strong and previously reviewed, but implementation still requires rendered-head, sitemap, route, responsive, accessibility, and production validation.

### 3. YMYL / legal-risk overstatement

Do not frame the entire article set as strict YMYL content or imply that WCAG creates a universal private-sector legal risk.

Maintain the existing boundary:

- official accessibility guidance;
- context-dependent legal obligations;
- general business information;
- qualified-counsel referral for material legal questions.

### 4. Unsupported search assertions

Do not adopt claims such as:

- "nobody in the market discusses operational capacity";
- "Google often shows PAA about translation cost";
- specific domain dominance;
- featured-snippet prevalence;
- AI Overview patterns;

unless supported by recorded queries, dates, markets, result examples, and links.

### 5. Article 2 RU sample legal wording

Reject the sentence that a business is "held hostage" when a transfer clause or registrar access is absent. It is rhetorically excessive and collapses multiple legal and operational conditions.

Preserve the more precise baseline language about rights, access, billing ownership, transfer path, and written copyright transfer.

### 6. Automatic use of React/Vue/MDX

Reject as an assumption.

Implementation must follow the repository's actual architecture. Premium article modules should first be designed for the existing stack and progressive enhancement.

### 7. Swipe-only mobile tables

Reject as the default.

Swipe-only interaction can reduce discoverability and accessibility. Prefer responsive cards, stacked labeled rows, or horizontal overflow with visible affordance, depending on the information structure.
