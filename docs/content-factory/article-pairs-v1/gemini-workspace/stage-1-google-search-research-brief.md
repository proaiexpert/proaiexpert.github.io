## The four search intents

### Article 1 RU

Help a Russian-speaking U.S. service-business owner choose among:

- English-only;
- selected Russian support;
- full RU/EN versions.

### Article 1 EN

Help a U.S. service business choose among:

- English-only disclosure;
- focused language coverage;
- full multilingual customer journeys.

### Article 2 RU

Help a Russian-speaking U.S. service-business owner evaluate a website provider and proposal before signing, with emphasis on reducing rework risk.

### Article 2 EN

Help a U.S. service business evaluate a website proposal through sales-to-signature reconciliation, risk classification, control, acceptance, and traceability.

## Research requirements

Use current Google Search results and current official documentation where applicable.

For each of the four intents, investigate:

1. Current SERP composition.
2. Dominant content types and publishers.
3. Searcher language and likely decision stage.
4. Common headings, questions, PAA patterns, and repeated subtopics.
5. Weaknesses in competing results.
6. Information gaps not adequately answered by the current SERP.
7. Query variants with the same intent.
8. Adjacent intents that must not be absorbed.
9. Metadata and title patterns.
10. Opportunities for stronger information gain.

## Google-specific verification

Verify only claims actually relevant to the articles:

- multilingual URL structure;
- `hreflang`;
- self-referencing and reciprocal annotations;
- locale-adaptive delivery;
- direct user access to language versions;
- title and description guidance;
- canonical and sitemap relationships;
- helpful-content principles;
- evidence placement and source quality.

Use official Google documentation for Google claims.

For WCAG, copyright, or domain claims, use the relevant primary official sources rather than secondary articles.

## Creative-opportunity research

Without redesigning the articles yet, identify opportunities for a more distinctive premium editorial experience:

- stronger opening tension;
- clearer named frameworks;
- decision tools;
- visual or interactive modules;
- premium article-page hierarchy;
- summary rails;
- evidence treatments;
- mobile-friendly table alternatives;
- useful calculators, checklists, or downloadable tools;
- section pacing and scannability;
- differentiated RU and EN storytelling.

Do not recommend decorative complexity without functional value.

## Guardrails

Do not:

- write replacement articles;
- propose keyword stuffing;
- invent search volume;
- invent performance data;
- add unsupported statistics;
- add unrelated AI Search chapters;
- turn Article 1 into generic multilingual software guidance;
- turn Article 2 into a legal memorandum or website-cost article;
- make RU and EN mechanical translations;
- rely on Financial Stream as performance evidence.
