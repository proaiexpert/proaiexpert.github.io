# Stage 2 Self-Evaluation V1

## Scoring Methodology
Scores are from 1 to 10. 
*   **Baseline** = `checkpoint-before-gemini` drafts.
*   **Candidate** = Stage 2 Creative Candidates.

---

### 1. Search-Intent Precision
*   **Baseline:** 9
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** The baseline already perfectly targeted the informational and commercial intents. The candidate maintains this exact focus without diluting it (no score change necessary).

### 2. Factual Discipline
*   **Baseline:** 10
*   **Candidate:** 10
*   **Justification:** All primary source facts (W3C, ICANN, Copyright Office, Google Search Central) were meticulously preserved and isolated into Authority Blocks. No degradation of facts.

### 3. Information Gain
*   **Baseline:** 8
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +1 due to the addition of the "Broken Journey" scenario and the specific CRM mapping (Entry -> Form -> CRM -> Response). This grounds abstract advice in highly specific operational examples that competitors lack.

### 4. Natural Language
*   **Baseline:** 6
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +3. The baseline suffered from "consulting template" syndrome—dry, repetitive, and slightly robotic. The candidate uses a much more human, decisive tone, framing the articles as strategic management briefings rather than data dumps.

### 5. Expert Authority (E-E-A-T)
*   **Baseline:** 8
*   **Candidate:** 10
*   **Justification:** Increased by +2. While the baseline had the facts, the candidate *displays* them like a premium publication through the "Authority Blocks" and "Executive Summaries," signaling high-tier consulting logic.

### 6. Readability
*   **Baseline:** 5
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +4. The baseline required users to read through seven identical markdown tables in a row (Article 2). The candidate transforms this into distinct, scannable frameworks, scenarios, and checklists.

### 7. Premium Editorial Quality
*   **Baseline:** 5
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +4. The introduction of "The Strategic System" blueprint (Badges, Executive Summaries, Scenario Cards, Decision Gates) elevates the article from a standard blog post to a proprietary tool.

### 8. Practical Usefulness
*   **Baseline:** 8
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +1. Replacing abstract tables with a 5-question Risk Ledger methodology (Article 2) gives the business owner a much easier framework to immediately apply to a PDF proposal they are currently reading.

### 9. Commercial Relevance
*   **Baseline:** 7
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +2. The candidate directly ties the website translation decision (Article 1) to CRM setup and automation routing, perfectly bridging ProAI Expert's "Websites" and "Automation" service verticals.

### 10. Mobile Suitability
*   **Baseline:** 4
*   **Candidate:** 9
*   **Justification:** Increased by +5. Massive markdown tables with 5-7 columns (Baseline Article 2) are notoriously hostile to mobile screens. The candidate transforms these into stacked lists and cards, removing horizontal scrolling risks.

### 11. Accessibility Feasibility
*   **Baseline:** 8
*   **Candidate:** 8
*   **Justification:** The baseline used standard markdown (highly accessible). The candidate relies on semantic HTML (`<aside>`, `<article>`, lists) keeping the accessibility score equally high. (No score change).

### 12. Implementation Complexity
*   **Baseline:** 2 (Very low - just render markdown)
*   **Candidate:** 4 
*   **Justification:** Increased by +2. The candidate requires custom CSS classes (e.g., `.scenario-card`, `.authority-block`) to achieve the premium visual hierarchy defined in the Blueprint. It is entirely achievable without JS, but requires more styling effort than raw markdown.
