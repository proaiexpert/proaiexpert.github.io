## 1. Purpose

This document preserves the strongest reusable art-direction, screenshot-presentation and motion principles derived from the Financial Stream flagship review process.

It is not a page-specific build task and does not replace a project Production Spec.

Its purpose is to ensure that future portfolio pages:

1. remain recognizably ProAI Expert;
2. feel premium without relying on decorative effects;
3. use real project evidence as the visual foundation;
4. share one coherent portfolio system while retaining project-specific identities;
5. work as complete desktop and mobile experiences;
6. remain accessible, performant and understandable without JavaScript enhancement.

---

## 2. Portfolio system principle

### Shared shell, different project worlds

All case pages share a recognizable ProAI framework:

- near-black and graphite global shell;
- restrained cyan as an active signal, not a decorative wash;
- large contemporary editorial typography;
- precise grid and spacing discipline;
- compact chapter orientation;
- real project captures as evidence;
- claim, source, status and limitation discipline;
- restrained, functional motion;
- no fake proof or invented business outcomes.

Each project then receives its own visual world, pacing and signature treatment.

The pages should feel related, not cloned.

## 3. Shared portfolio visual grammar

### 3.1 Grid

Use a strict responsive layout grid as structural infrastructure.

Desktop default:

- 12 columns;
- project-specific asymmetry;
- precise alignment between heading, proof surface, caption and source line;
- local datum marks where they clarify evidence relationships.

Mobile default:

- 4 columns;
- single-column narrative flow for complex chapters;
- no page-level horizontal overflow;
- no mandatory horizontal gallery.

The grid should be felt through composition. It should not become a permanently visible technical background.

### 3.2 Typography

Use typography to create scale, rhythm and hierarchy rather than animation for its own sake.

Shared rules:

- one clear H1;
- natural language-specific line wrapping;
- RU, UA and EN layouts do not require identical height;
- limited monospace use for source/status metadata only;
- no tiny uppercase labels with excessive tracking;
- captions remain readable and adjacent to their evidence;
- major chapters vary in scale and density.

### 3.3 Color

Shared ProAI colors:

- near-black;
- graphite;
- ice-white;
- restrained cyan.

Project-specific color worlds may enter inside the case canvas.

Cyan is reserved for:

- current alignment;
- active chapter orientation;
- a verified connection;
- a controlled CTA emphasis.

Green is reserved for confirmed operational status. It is not a general premium accent.

### 3.4 Surface hierarchy

Use a limited number of surface roles:

1. ProAI black shell.
2. Project dark surface.
3. Project light/documentary surface.
4. Proof surface for real screenshots or evidence.

Do not invent a new surface recipe for every chapter.

## 4. Screenshot presentation system

### 4.1 Core decision

Real screenshots are presented as evidence surfaces, not as decorative device mockups.

Do not use:

- Apple MacBook frames;
- iPhone hardware frames;
- generic laptop or phone mockups;
- fake browser chrome;
- floating 3D devices;
- device tilt;
- decorative screen reflections;
- hardware branding that implies an unrelated product association.

Reasons:

- device mockups make real work look like a template presentation;
- they reduce the readable area of the actual interface;
- they distract from evidence and system logic;
- they age faster than a neutral editorial presentation;
- they create a generic agency aesthetic shared by thousands of portfolio pages.

### 4.2 Desktop screenshots

Desktop screenshots may be presented as:

- near-full-bleed proof surfaces;
- large controlled crops;
- editorial panels aligned to text and source metadata;
- edge-reaching compositions that extend toward the viewport boundary.

They may use:

- one restrained proof mat;
- one thin border;
- a small radius;
- a restrained surface-specific shadow.

They must not combine mat, glass, glow, thick border, large radius and heavy shadow simultaneously.

### 4.3 Mobile screenshots

Portrait captures are shown as real full-frame screens without an iPhone body.

Allowed treatment:

- full portrait ratio;
- restrained 8–10 px corner radius when consistent with the case system;
- one thin border or proof mat;
- route-matched language first;
- shared caption and source rail for bilingual pairs.

Wide desktop screenshots on mobile use a controlled focal proof window rather than being reduced to an unreadable strip.

The complete capture must remain accessible through a normal link or expanded figure treatment when a focal crop is used.

### 4.4 Screenshot scales

Use only three main scales.

#### Scale A — Flagship proof

For the strongest project-defining screens.

- 8–12 columns;
- controlled crop permitted;
- dominant composition;
- limited to a small number of moments per case.

#### Scale B — Major proof panel

For an important workflow or service-path screen.

- 7–9 columns;
- paired with explanatory process text;
- normal flow by default;
- sticky only when the project-specific Production Spec proves that it improves understanding.

#### Scale C — Editorial comparison

For a bilingual pair, responsive pair or structured comparison.

- shared proof surface;
- common baseline and caption;
- no separate generic cards;
- no fake devices.

### 4.5 Screenshot readability gate

A screenshot cannot carry a primary claim when its important internal UI becomes unreadable.

At every required viewport, verify that:

- the main heading remains identifiable;
- the relevant CTA or field remains visible;
- chat or form content does not become an indistinct texture;
- the screenshot still proves the captioned claim.

When it fails:

- increase the proof surface;
- use a controlled crop;
- provide a textual equivalent;
- or reduce the screenshot to a supporting role.

## 5. Evidence presentation system

### 5.1 Shared logic

Every important proof moment should answer:

1. What is being claimed?
2. What real visual or source supports it?
3. Which route, language, date or system produced it?
4. What is its implementation status?
5. What does it not prove?

### 5.2 Evidence Reconciliation Register

The reusable Financial Stream-originated system is called:

**Evidence Reconciliation Register / Реестр сверки доказательств**

It may be used when a project depends on evidence, status and operational boundaries.

A local register can contain:

- chapter or claim index;
- verified claim;
- proof surface;
- route / language / date;
- implementation status;
- limitation.

The register is a local alignment and information system.

It is not:

- a continuous animated line through the full page;
- a decorative ledger grid;
- a fintech dashboard;
- a collection of tiny labels;
- a universal signature forced onto every project.

### 5.3 Status language

Reusable capability statuses:

- `LIVE`;
- `IMPLEMENTED · HUMAN REVIEW`;
- `TESTED / PARTIAL`;
- `PLANNED / UNVERIFIED`.

Status is attached to a specific capability, not automatically to an entire chapter or project.

## 6. Shared motion discipline

### 6.1 Motion purpose

Motion may clarify:

- alignment;
- hierarchy;
- sequence;
- chapter change;
- proof fixation;
- system reconciliation;
- final action.

Motion must not:

- create essential content;
- delay access to evidence;
- simulate interactivity that does not exist;
- turn the page into an animation demo;
- repeat identically on every paragraph or card.

### 6.2 Shared permitted motion vocabulary

Preferred reusable effects:

1. small proof-surface settle;
2. local registration/alignment lock;
3. one project-specific system reconciliation effect;
4. confined dark/light surface handoff;
5. source/status fixation;
6. restrained final CTA closure.

Typical movement limits:

- 4–12 px translation;
- opacity begins near visible, generally not below 0.94 for essential large visuals;
- no large scale changes;
- no blur/filter motion on large raster images;
- no continuous scroll-linked interpolation.

Preferred expressive easing:

`cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`

### 6.3 Shared prohibited motion

Do not use:

- scroll-jacking;
- mandatory smooth scrolling;
- horizontal scroll storytelling;
- animated number counting;
- large screenshot parallax;
- focus pull or blur transitions;
- perpetual background movement;
- cyan glow trails;
- orbiting nodes or particles;
- cursor-following effects;
- typing simulations;
- repeated scanlines;
- 3D page flips or device rotations;
- animation on every heading, paragraph and list;
- motion that prevents immediate access under reduced motion or no JavaScript.

### 6.4 Accessibility and fallbacks

Every project Production Spec must define:

- reduced-motion behavior;
- no-JavaScript final state;
- sticky disable conditions;
- focus visibility and focus-obscuration checks;
- complete content without hover;
- mobile and zoom/reflow behavior.

## 7. Shared chapter rhythm principles

A long case page must not repeat:

`heading → paragraph → screenshot → caption`

for every chapter.

Each case should contain:

- one clear opening;
- one conceptual climax;
- one or two major real-proof moments;
- text-only chapters;
- at least one quiet human or reflective pause;
- one strict evidence chapter;
- one decisive final CTA.

Rules:

- no three consecutive chapters with the same composition;
- two large screenshots should not appear back-to-back without a density change;
- a major visual moment should be followed by a calmer chapter;
- sticky storytelling is limited to one chapter unless a later owner-approved Production Spec explicitly justifies an exception;
- short chapters are not artificially stretched to 100vh;
- desktop and mobile may use different pacing while retaining the same factual sequence.

## 9. Case Studies archive direction

The archive may use a stronger portfolio-level transition system than individual cases, but it must remain controlled.

Recommended direction:

- large editorial project stages rather than a small generic card grid;
- one dominant real image per project;
- project-specific color world enters locally;
- shared project status and truth labels;
- restrained project transition;
- no fake devices;
- no autoplay video requirement;
- no blur-to-sharp or animated counters as default behavior;
- no stacked sticky cards on mobile;
- all essential information visible without hover.

The archive should introduce each project world. It should not repeat the full signature effect of each case page.

---

## 10. Premiumity killers

Avoid across the portfolio:

- generic glassmorphism;
- identical rounded cards everywhere;
- excessive pills;
- decorative cyan on every component;
- large device mockups;
- browser chrome;
- small unreadable metadata;
- repeated section reveal animation;
- long empty spaces without narrative function;
- dashboard-like metric tiles;
- generic AI imagery;
- fake proof;
- identical page choreography for every project;
- a Notion-like sequence of text cards and screenshots;
- effects that become the subject instead of the real work.

---

## 11. Required project-specific Production Spec

Before implementation, every case must receive a project-specific Production Spec defining:

1. project truth and claim boundaries;
2. final chapter order;
3. exact public copy by language;
4. screenshot paths and route usage;
5. crop and readability rules;
6. desktop/tablet/mobile composition;
7. project-specific signature system;
8. exact approved effects and timings;
9. reduced-motion/no-JavaScript behavior;
10. accessibility and performance criteria;
11. complete-page preview requirements;
12. owner approval gate.

This shared document never authorizes implementation by itself.

---

## 12. Implementation boundary

- Do not edit `main` directly.
- Work only on the approved portfolio branch.
- Do not publish, merge or create a PR without owner instruction.
- Do not send a project to Codex implementation until its Production Spec is complete and owner-approved.
- Browser/source recovery and production code remain Codex tasks when technically necessary.
- Strategy, copy, evidence control and documentation remain controlled through the portfolio planning process.
