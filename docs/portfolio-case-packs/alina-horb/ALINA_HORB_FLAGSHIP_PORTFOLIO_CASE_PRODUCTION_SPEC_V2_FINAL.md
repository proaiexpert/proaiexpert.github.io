- Must the request already be precisely formulated?
- Can the visitor first understand the approach through expert material?

## 01. The brief

**Eyebrow:** `THE BRIEF`  
**Title:** `A personal practice needed more than a brochure.`

Alina’s practice already had a clear voice and way of working. It needed a digital structure capable of introducing the person, explaining the service and guiding a visitor towards first contact.

The task was therefore to build a complete product—not arrange a portrait, biography and booking button on a template.

**Caption**  
`The homepage connects personal presence, professional context and the next step.`

## 02. The template risk

**Eyebrow:** `THE RISK`  
**Title:** `Trust disappears when the specialist becomes interchangeable.`

Generic therapist websites often rely on the same imagery, promises and service cards. They may look polished while leaving the specialist difficult to remember.

Differentiation had to come from Alina’s own presence, language, boundaries and way of organising the consultation experience.

**Pull line**  
`The practice needed to feel personal and clear—not louder.`

## 03. Trust strategy

**Eyebrow:** `TRUST STRATEGY`  
**Title:** `Clarity comes before persuasion.`

The experience follows the questions a visitor may have before contacting a psychologist: who they are speaking to, how the process works, what happens after a message and whether a perfect request is necessary.

Personal context comes before conditions; conditions come before the form. Notes offer another way to understand Alina’s approach before making contact.

**Caption**  
`The information hierarchy supports informed choice instead of pressure.`

## 04. Personal brand

**Eyebrow:** `ART DIRECTION`  
**Title:** `A visual identity with a human centre.`

Premium Editorial Sanctuary combines ivory, warm stone, muted sage, restrained terracotta, serif typography and recurring arches.

Portraits establish authorship and presence. The About experience connects that presence with professional context without becoming a résumé or credential wall.

**Caption**  
`Identity is expressed through proportion, rhythm and presence rather than therapist clichés.`

## 05. Bilingual architecture

**Eyebrow:** `UA / RU ARCHITECTURE`  
**Title:** `Two languages. One coherent practice.`

Ukrainian and Russian are complete corresponding experiences across the homepage, About, Consultations, Notes, four articles and Privacy.

**Title:** `Understand the process before making contact.`

A dedicated route explains the path from a short message to the first consultation, including format, practical conditions and professional boundaries.

**Supporting line**
