## Main questions to resolve

### A. Opening experience

1. What exactly appears in the first viewport before scrolling?
2. Does the hero show a full screenshot, a controlled crop, a paired composition or delayed visual entry?
3. What is the exact order of title, status, thesis, proof facts and first visual?
4. What should the user understand within the first five seconds?
5. What happens during the first two meaningful scroll movements?
6. Which elements are static, sticky, revealed or transformed?
7. How should the RU opening adapt to longer text without becoming a compromised copy of EN?

### B. Twelve-chapter production sequence

For every chapter, determine:

- exact purpose;
- user question answered;
- recommended headline direction;
- required text depth;
- exact visual/evidence asset;
- desktop composition;
- mobile composition;
- caption and claim relationship;
- whether the chapter is dark, light or transitional;
- interaction behavior;
- exit into the next chapter.

The twelve chapters are:

1. Hero.
2. Proof strip.
3. Business challenge.
4. Five-layer system architecture.
5. Bilingual experience.
6. Service architecture.
7. Intake before booking.
8. Content and search foundation.
9. AI and automation status.
10. Owner testimonial.
11. Verified outcomes.
12. Live project and ProAI CTA.

### C. Screenshot placement

Resolve exact use of:

- EN/RU homepage hero captures;
- EN/RU Request V2 captures;
- EN/RU Reporting + Chatbase V2 captures;
- EN/RU Materials desktop captures;
- EN/RU Company Formation mobile portraits;
- any approved redacted GSC or automation evidence defined in the evidence index.

For each asset decide:

- public use or reserve;
- chapter;
- exact role;
- full frame or crop;
- single, paired, overlay or comparison treatment;
- caption;
- verified claim supported;
- desktop width and alignment;
- tablet behavior;
- mobile behavior;
- whether EN and RU versions should use language-matched assets or show a deliberate bilingual comparison.

Avoid repetitive consecutive language duplicates.

### D. Copy and descriptions

Provide exact recommended copy foundation for both EN and RU:

- hero eyebrow;
- H1;
- hero thesis;
- project-status labels;
- proof-strip labels;
- twelve section titles;
- one concise lead paragraph per section;
- all screenshot captions;
- metric/evidence captions;
- automation status labels;
- testimonial introduction;
- final CTA title, supporting line and button labels.

RU copy must sound natural for a Russian-speaking business owner. It must not be a mechanical translation of EN.

Do not invent long-form facts beyond the controlling sources. Mark any sentence that still requires owner confirmation.

### E. Motion and interaction

Specify only motion that materially improves comprehension:

- trigger;
- duration;
- easing;
- start and end state;
- desktop/tablet/mobile behavior;
- reduced-motion fallback;
- no-JS fallback;
- performance risk.

Decide whether the proposed chapter rail, sticky panels, evidence spine, reconciliation stack, dark/light transitions and screenshot reveals should be retained, simplified or removed.

No permanent ambient animation. No interaction may hide essential content.

### F. Responsive production behavior

Define the page at:

- large desktop;
- standard desktop;
- tablet landscape;
- tablet portrait;
- mobile portrait.

Resolve:

- maximum text widths;
- screenshot readability;
- paired-language collapse rules;
- chapter navigation on mobile;
- sticky behavior removal thresholds;
- proof-strip wrapping;
- captions;
- metric cards;
- testimonial layout;
- final CTA.

### G. Commercial clarity and evidence

Determine whether the sequence proves all of the following without exaggeration:

- a real live client project;
- bilingual service architecture;
- structured intake before booking;
- content/search foundation;
- human-reviewed automation;
- dated search visibility evidence;
- ongoing optimization.

Identify any section that feels decorative, repetitive, too technical or commercially unclear.
