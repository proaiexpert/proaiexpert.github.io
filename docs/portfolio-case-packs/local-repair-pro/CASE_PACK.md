## 4. Target Market and Audience

### Primary business profile

Проект разработан для небольшого или среднего local handyman / home-repair business, который:

- выполняет малые и средние домашние задачи;
- не хочет позиционироваться как крупный general contractor;
- работает с homeowners;
- принимает переменные по объёму запросы;
- должен заранее понимать тип задачи, location, materials и access;
- хочет уменьшить количество бессодержательных звонков;
- хочет собирать фотографии и детали до scheduling.

### Primary audience

Homeowners в Vancouver, Clark County и соседних Portland-area communities, которым нужны:

- небольшие ремонты;
- touch-ups;
- trim and hardware fixes;
- wall repair;
- small installations;
- TV mounting;
- fixture replacement review;
- несколько мелких задач в одном punch-list.

### Audience characteristics

Пользователь сайта:

- часто не знает точного названия услуги;
- может не понимать, нужен ли handyman или licensed specialist;
- хочет быстро узнать, подходит ли его задача;
- предпочитает отправить фотографии вместо длинного телефонного разговора;
- хочет видеть понятный next step;
- не хочет сразу проходить сложный booking flow;
- хочет заранее понимать service area и ограничения.

### Market segment

Визуально сайт позиционируется как premium-leaning local service, но подтверждённого price point или фактического premium pricing нет.

Правильная формулировка:

> Premium presentation and organized service experience for a local handyman business.

Неправильная формулировка:

> Premium-priced handyman service.

## 5. Business Challenge

### Core challenge

Типичный handyman website часто представляет собой:

- общий список из десятков услуг;
- телефон без предварительной квалификации;
- шаблонные заявления licensed, insured, top-rated;
- неясную service area;
- непонятный pricing;
- слабую мобильную навигацию;
- отсутствие photo guidance;
- отсутствие понятного процесса;
- слишком общий CTA `Call now`;
- неструктурированные обращения без location, materials и scope.

### Project objective

Создать premium-looking, но понятный массовому homeowner сайт, который:

1. показывает типы задач;
2. объясняет ограничения scope;
3. помогает посетителю определить fit;
4. предлагает отправить детали и фото;
5. собирает необходимые данные до scheduling;
6. снижает количество неподходящих обращений;
7. помогает владельцу бизнеса быстрее оценивать request quality.

### Desired visitor action

Главное действие:

**Request Estimate**

Вторичное действие:

**Send Photos**

Поддерживающие действия:

- Call;
- Text;
- View Services;
- View Work Examples;
- View Service Area;
- read FAQ;
- review Pricing Approach.

### Information needed before estimate or call

- name — optional;
- best contact method;
- city or ZIP;
- request type;
- location of the issue inside/outside the home;
- description;
- preferred timing;
- whether several tasks are grouped;
- photo context;
- access notes;
- materials or fixture details.

### Commercial purpose

Вместо неподготовленного звонка сайт должен создавать более качественный request:

> service type + location + photos + access + materials + timing + scope.

## 6. Market Research Findings

### Confirmed research direction

В проектном контексте была сформирована задача изучить:

- top handyman websites in the United States;
- разные pricing and presentation tiers;
- 2–3 strong premium examples;
- 2–3 simple examples;
- около четырёх middle-market examples;
- 2–4 direct local competitors для глубокого UX review.

Однако точный список названий и URL проанализированных конкурентов в доступных материалах не сохранён. Поэтому конкретные competitor brands нельзя включать в публичный кейс без повторного подтверждения.

### Strategic concepts considered

На раннем этапе рассматривались три направления:

1. Reliable Local Repair Pro
2. Premium Home Maintenance Concierge
3. Fast Fix / Same-Week Handyman

Выбранное направление:

**Premium Local Repair Pro — Pacific Northwest Edition**

### Strong market approaches identified

- finished-result hero instead of showing damage;
- local geography above the fold;
- photo-based estimate flow;
- structured service categories;
- clear service-area expectations;
- mobile conversion controls;
- process clarity;
- useful FAQ;
- pricing explanation without misleading fixed quotes;
- visual examples organized by request type;
- homeowner-focused copy;
- clear differentiation between handyman scope and licensed-trade work.

### Common market weaknesses identified

- generic stock-template appearance;
- broad and unfocused service lists;
- weak visual hierarchy;
- fake or unverified trust claims;
- unclear geographical coverage;
- fake urgency and same-day promises;
- no qualification before calls;
- no guidance on what photos to send;
- project galleries presented without context;
- poor mobile headers and CTA overlap;
- no explanation of how estimates are reviewed.

### Approaches adopted

- premium finished-value hero;
- scope-reviewed positioning;
- service categories instead of one long list;
- photo-first intake;
- city/service-area architecture;
- mobile sticky CTA;
- demo-labeled visual scenarios;
- process transparency;
- safe specialist disclaimers;
- noindex while facts are unverified.

### Approaches consciously rejected

- hero image focused on damage or defects;
- fake reviews;
- fake completed-project portfolio;
- fake license or insurance claims;
- fake exact service radius;
- fake Google Maps location;
- instant estimate language;
- guaranteed response-time language;
- same-day promises;
- fixed pricing before verification;
- LocalBusiness schema with placeholders;
- restoring obsolete preview routes;
- presenting sample visuals as real completed work.

### External advisory ideas reviewed but not implemented

The following improvements were discussed as possible future premium upgrades:

- interactive before/after slider;
- subtle hero micro-video;
- white-glove protocol section;
- work-session formats / half-day and full-day framing;
- more interactive Photo Estimate guide.

They remain planned concepts, not completed features.

## 8. Website Architecture

### Current primary pages

| Page | Commercial function |
|---|---|
| Homepage `/` | Establishes positioning, location, service fit and main CTA |
| Services `/services/` | Organizes request categories and helps visitors identify fit |
| Work Examples `/work-examples/` | Demonstrates example project types without claiming real completed work |

Homepage → FAQ / Guides / Services → Quick Question → request fit clarification → future follow-up.

1. Repair Request

- name — optional;

- city or ZIP;
- request type;
- issue location;
- description;
- timing;
- grouped punch-list indicator;

- close-up photos;
- measurements or scale references;

## 13. Key Strategic Decisions

### Decision 1 — Finished-value hero

**Problem:** damage-focused hero images create anxiety and make the brand look like a low-cost repair listing.

**Solution:** use a finished premium PNW entryway.

**Logic:** show the quality of the desired result, not the defect.

**Commercial function:** supports premium positioning and homeowner confidence.

**Status:** implemented.

### Decision 2 — Photo-based estimate process

**Problem:** phone-only intake produces unclear requests and unnecessary back-and-forth.

**Solution:** ask visitors to describe the project and provide photo context.

**Logic:** photos clarify size, materials, access and condition.

**Commercial function:** improves request quality before scheduling.

**Status:** guidance implemented; real upload and processing not implemented.

### Decision 3 — Two-path intake

**Problem:** one long form creates too much friction for uncertain visitors.

**Solution:** provide Quick Question and Repair Request.

**Logic:** low-intent visitors can ask a short question; ready visitors can provide full scope.

**Commercial function:** captures more visitors without sacrificing qualification.

**Status:** UI implemented; real endpoint not implemented.

### Decision 4 — Service-area clarity without false guarantees

**Problem:** exact-radius promises may be inaccurate for a concept business.

**Solution:** use Vancouver-first and scope-reviewed area language.

**Logic:** availability can depend on travel, parking, access and project type.

**Commercial function:** creates local relevance without overpromising.

**Status:** implemented.

### Decision 5 — Scenario gallery instead of fake portfolio

**Problem:** no verified completed-project photography exists.

**Solution:** use sample repair scenarios with explicit demo labeling.

**Logic:** visitors still understand the work categories without being misled.

**Commercial function:** provides visual service education.

**Status:** implemented.

### Decision 6 — Process clarity instead of fake credentials

**Problem:** the concept cannot honestly claim license, insurance, reviews or years of experience.

**Solution:** build trust through transparent process and scope language.

**Logic:** clear expectations are both useful and defensible.

**Commercial function:** builds credibility while preserving factual integrity.

**Status:** implemented.

### Decision 7 — Pricing approach, not fixed pricing

**Problem:** handyman requests vary heavily by materials, access, timing and scope.

**Solution:** explain estimate factors and why there is no public fixed price.

**Logic:** avoids misleading quote expectations.

**Commercial function:** prepares users to provide better details.

**Status:** implemented.

### Decision 8 — Mobile persistent actions

**Problem:** mobile visitors can lose access to contact actions while scrolling.

**Solution:** use a sticky Call / Text / Estimate dock.

**Logic:** local-service leads frequently originate on mobile.

**Commercial function:** keeps conversion actions accessible.

**Status:** implemented; final live regression still required.

### Decision 9 — Local content architecture

**Problem:** a single generic service-area sentence provides weak local relevance.

**Solution:** build a service-area hub and city pages.

**Logic:** each area can address local access, parking, travel and scope considerations.

**Commercial function:** strengthens local navigation and future SEO landing-page capability.

**Status:** implemented but intentionally noindexed.

### Decision 10 — Automation-ready, not automation-faked

**Problem:** the concept needs to demonstrate future operational depth without pretending integrations exist.

**Solution:** build structured form and field architecture first.

**Logic:** the same data can later feed email, CRM, SMS or AI workflows.

**Commercial function:** makes the website a foundation for a larger lead-management system.

**Status:** form architecture implemented; automations planned.
