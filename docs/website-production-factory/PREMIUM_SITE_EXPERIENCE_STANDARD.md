# Premium Site Experience Standard

**Status:** Canonical Website Production Factory standard  
**Prepared:** 2026-07-24  
**Applies to:** Future ProAI Expert website projects, niche templates, demos, client sites, and production case extraction

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the reusable premium experience standard for:

- local-service websites;
- professional services;
- accounting and financial services;
- medical and dental organizations;
- expert-led personal brands;
- future ProAI Expert niche templates.

It supplements the factory pipeline, QA protocols, local-service guardrails, and agent workflow. It does not replace niche research, client truth, legal review, or project-specific art direction.

Premium quality is not a visual style. It is the disciplined alignment of strategy, information architecture, proof, interaction, responsive behavior, accessibility, performance, and truthful claims.

---

## 2. Strategic foundation

Before design or implementation, define:

1. **Business context** — what the business actually does and does not do.
2. **Audience** — who must understand, trust, and act.
3. **Core trust problem** — what uncertainty prevents the next step.
4. **Decision friction** — what the visitor does not know yet.
5. **Content architecture** — what sequence resolves that uncertainty.
6. **First meaningful action** — the smallest useful next step.
7. **Available proof** — what can be shown and verified.
8. **Unavailable proof** — what must not be implied.
9. **Legal and claim boundaries** — regulated, licensed, clinical, financial, local-service, or performance constraints.
10. **Language scope** — routes, metadata, localization, and ownership.

Do not begin premium art direction until these inputs are sufficiently stable.

---

## 3. Strategy before surface

Required order:

1. Positioning.
2. Audience and intent.
3. Trust problem.
4. Information architecture.
5. Conversion and first-step logic.
6. Proof model.
7. Copy strategy.
8. Visual direction.
9. Motion direction.
10. Implementation and QA.

Rules:

- no styling before content structure;
- no Hero before positioning;
- no CTA hierarchy before user intent;
- no proof treatment before proof verification;
- no visual direction without audience and competitor context;
- no motion concept before the static hierarchy works;
- no component extraction before the project-specific version is proven.

---

## 4. Trust architecture

Each project must identify a primary trust type and any supporting trust types.

### 4.1 Operational trust

The visitor must understand scope, process, capability, status, and responsibility.

### 4.2 Personal trust

The visitor must understand the person, approach, boundaries, and tone before contact.

### 4.3 Practical service trust

The visitor must understand service fit, geography, project context, preparation, and next step.

### 4.4 Clinical or regulated trust

The site must distinguish information, professional scope, emergency boundaries, consent, privacy, and prohibited outcome claims.

### 4.5 Local-market trust

The site must communicate relevant geography and service logic without inventing coverage, reviews, local history, or operating facts.

### 4.6 Technical or system trust

The site must make system boundaries, automation status, human review, security assumptions, and implementation status visible where relevant.

A website may involve several trust types, but one primary trust problem should govern the experience.

---

## 5. Signature system requirement

Each premium project should contain one meaningful signature system.

Examples:

- structured intake logic;
- bilingual alignment;
- service-selection system;
- evidence register;
- photo guidance;
- editorial threshold;
- responsive reflow visualization;
- capability status map;
- process alignment.

The signature system must:

- clarify the offer, decision, workflow, proof, or customer journey;
- be understandable without animation;
- remain useful on mobile;
- be truthful;
- have a clear role in the narrative;
- be specific enough to be memorable.

It must not be:

- decorative branding without functional value;
- a fake software interface;
- a fabricated metric;
- an effect that obscures content;
- a reused motif that does not fit the current project.

## 6. Evidence and claim discipline

### 6.1 Evidence classes

Use explicit classes:

- verified current fact;
- owner-provided fact;
- implementation fact;
- live route or live feature;
- tested capability;
- partial capability;
- planned capability;
- concept or in-development work;
- dated external evidence;
- limitation.

### 6.2 Evidence rules

- date evidence when timing matters;
- name the source category;
- explain what the evidence proves;
- explain what it does not prove;
- separate implementation from business outcomes;
- separate tested from operational;
- separate a concept from a client engagement;
- preserve human-review boundaries.

### 6.3 Prohibited implications

Do not invent or imply:

- reviews;
- customer counts;
- conversion lift;
- revenue lift;
- guaranteed rankings;
- leads;
- licenses;
- insurance;
- warranties;
- years in business;
- service radius;
- clinical outcomes;
- financial outcomes;
- AI autonomy;
- completed client status for a concept.

## 7. Screenshot and proof hierarchy

Every screenshot must have a job.

### 7.1 Hero proof

Shows the strongest opening relationship between positioning, identity, and action.

### 7.2 Signature proof

Shows the project-specific system that makes the work memorable.

### 7.3 Operational proof

Shows how the website or system works beyond the homepage.

### 7.4 Responsive proof

Shows changed hierarchy and usable content, not merely a device frame.

### 7.5 Closing proof

Echoes the strongest project idea and supports the final next step.

### 7.6 Selection rules

- prefer deeper system proof when available;
- avoid showing only homepage screenshots;
- do not use multiple near-duplicate captures;
- use real captures over decorative mockups;
- use device frames only when the device context is relevant;
- keep captions concise and specific;
- include source or capture date when material;
- do not crop out evidence required to understand the screenshot;
- preserve readable text where text is the proof;
- provide optimized responsive variants.

### 7.7 Reuse limits

A screenshot may appear in a Hero and one supporting context only when the compositions communicate different information. Repeated use without new meaning reduces proof value.

---

## 8. Editorial composition and rhythm

Premium long-form pages require deliberate rhythm.

### 8.1 Reading measure

Keep body copy within a readable line length. Do not allow large screens to create overly wide paragraphs.

### 8.2 Density variation

Alternate:

- concise thesis sections;
- proof-led sections;
- explanatory sections;
- visual pauses;
- synthesis sections;
- closing sections.

### 8.3 Breakout proof fields

Key screenshots may extend beyond the text measure when the wider scale improves evidence. They must still align to the page grid and remain readable on mobile.

### 8.4 Surface rhythm

Plan light, dark, warm, cool, quiet, and proof surfaces before implementation. Surface change should mark narrative change.

### 8.5 Avoid repetition

Do not stack:

- equal cards;
- repeated numbered lists;
- identical screenshot modules;
- multiple disclaimers stating the same limitation;
- long sections with the same visual weight.

### 8.6 Chapter openings and closings

Each major chapter should establish:

- what question it answers;
- what evidence it uses;
- what conclusion the visitor should retain.

---

## 9. Surface planning

Create a surface map before coding.

Typical roles:

1. **Opening surface** — positioning and primary proof.
2. **Contrast surface** — challenge, risk, or alternative.
3. **Proof surface** — screenshot, evidence, system, or process.
4. **Human surface** — person, owner perspective, principles, or context.
5. **Closing surface** — synthesis and next step.

Rules:

- transitions support hierarchy;
- dark-to-light changes require intentional handoff;
- do not alternate surfaces mechanically;
- do not use surface variety to hide weak content;
- ensure contrast and focus visibility on every surface.

## 10. Motion standard

Motion must reveal hierarchy, relationship, state, or sequence.

### 10.1 Required properties

- understandable static state;
- bounded duration;
- restrained distance;
- predictable easing;
- run-once behavior for scroll reveals;
- no layout instability;
- no content dependence;
- no focus or keyboard obstruction.

### 10.2 Typical limits

- movement: normally 8–16 px;
- duration: normally 240–650 ms;
- simultaneous prominent effects: no more than two;
- repeat only when interaction state genuinely changes.

### 10.3 Prohibited patterns

- scroll-jacking;
- continuous parallax;
- decorative particles;
- cursor trails;
- autoplay storytelling;
- long intro sequences;
- blur-dependent content;
- animated counters without verified value;
- typing effects;
- 3D spectacle without product meaning;
- motion that delays navigation.

### 10.4 Reduced motion

`prefers-reduced-motion` must:

- remove nonessential movement;
- preserve content order and state;
- keep focus and interaction feedback;
- avoid replacing motion with abrupt layout changes.

### 10.5 No-JS baseline

Core content, navigation, proof, and CTA must remain visible and usable without JavaScript.

## 11. Responsive re-composition

Mobile is not reduced desktop.

For each major component, specify content order at:

- large desktop;
- laptop;
- tablet;
- mobile;
- narrow mobile.

Define explicitly:

- H1 position;
- proof position;
- CTA position;
- navigation position;
- status position;
- caption position;
- content priority;
- acceptable cropping;
- hidden decorative elements;
- elements that must never be removed.

### 11.1 Mobile requirements

- primary meaning appears early;
- primary action remains visible without dominating;
- screenshots remain interpretable;
- tables and matrices recompose rather than simply shrink;
- sticky elements do not consume excessive viewport;
- tap targets remain sufficient;
- no horizontal overflow;
- motion is reduced when complexity would harm usability.

### 11.2 Required narrow-width checks

At minimum review:

- 430 px;
- 390 px;
- 375 px;
- 360 px;
- 320 px.

## 12. Bilingual and multilingual systems

A multilingual site is a route and content system, not a translation widget.

Require:

- separate routes;
- natural localized copy;
- localized titles and descriptions;
- self-canonical URLs;
- reciprocal hreflang;
- x-default;
- language-preserving navigation;
- exact page-to-page language mapping;
- localized structured data where applicable;
- comparable proof quality;
- mobile parity;
- form and success-state parity;
- sitemap inclusion;
- no mechanical translation.

Do not describe a UA/RU site as EN/RU merely because the portfolio wrapper is English and Russian.

## 13. Accessibility

Required:

- semantic heading hierarchy;
- landmarks;
- skip link;
- meaningful link text;
- visible focus;
- complete keyboard interaction;
- sufficient contrast;
- meaningful alt text;
- decorative image suppression where appropriate;
- labels and instructions for forms;
- adequate touch targets;
- reduced motion;
- no information encoded only by color;
- no information encoded only by motion;
- sensible reading order;
- accessible disclosure and accordion behavior;
- forced-colors review where relevant.

Accessibility must be checked in rendered pages, not inferred from source alone.

---

## 14. Performance

Set a performance budget before visual polish expands.

### 14.1 Core targets

Project-specific targets may be stricter, but the default production objective is:

- LCP below 2.5 seconds in representative mobile testing;
- CLS near zero;
- TBT near zero for static sites;
- no unnecessary blocking scripts;
- no duplicate asset requests.

### 14.2 Images

- preload only the true LCP image;
- use responsive image variants;
- declare dimensions;
- lazy-load below-fold images;
- use modern formats;
- avoid oversized mobile downloads;
- verify crop and text readability.

### 14.3 Fonts

- minimize font families and weights;
- use appropriate display behavior;
- avoid loading decorative fonts without material value.

### 14.4 JavaScript

- progressive enhancement only;
- avoid libraries for effects achievable with small native code;
- defer noncritical scripts;
- remove listeners and observers when not needed;
- do not make basic content visibility depend on JavaScript.

### 14.5 Third parties

Every third-party script requires a documented purpose, privacy consideration, failure behavior, and performance cost.

---

## 15. Navigation and closing

### 15.1 Orientation

Visitors should always understand:

- where they are;
- what the page proves;
- what section they are reading;
- where the next meaningful path leads.

### 15.2 Contextual next step

The CTA should follow the page argument. Avoid repeating the same generic CTA without context.

### 15.3 Relationship network

Connect:

- service to relevant case;
- case to relevant service;
- article to relevant case or service;
- archive to project;
- project to archive;
- project to next project;
- contact path to the context that generated it.

### 15.4 External links

External destinations must be visually and semantically clear.

### 15.5 Closing quality

A strong closing:

- restates the project’s distinctive value;
- preserves truthful limitations;
- provides one primary next step;
- avoids adding a new argument after the conclusion;
- transitions coherently into the global footer.

---

## 16. Production extraction

After launch or approved testbed completion, extract:

- reusable information architecture;
- niche-specific patterns;
- successful signature systems;
- failed experiments;
- proof selection lessons;
- motion lessons;
- responsive lessons;
- accessibility findings;
- performance findings;
- claim risks;
- localization findings;
- prompt and task improvements;
- template changes.

Do not copy client facts, testimonials, photos, claims, or outcomes into another project.

---

## 17. Acceptance checklist

Before approval, confirm:

### Strategy

- primary audience and trust problem are explicit;
- first meaningful action is clear;
- proof and claim boundaries are documented.

### Experience

- signature system has functional value;
- page rhythm is varied and intentional;
- Hero, proof, and closing form one argument.

### Truth

- all claims have a source class;
- concept, tested, partial, live, and planned states are distinct;
- prohibited implications are absent.

### Responsive

- desktop, tablet, mobile, and narrow mobile are intentionally composed;
- required widths pass without overflow.

### Accessibility

- keyboard, focus, contrast, headings, landmarks, alt text, reduced motion, and forms pass rendered review.

### Performance

- LCP image, responsive images, layout stability, script cost, and third-party requests pass the project budget.

### Localization

- route, metadata, canonical, hreflang, x-default, content, proof, forms, and mobile parity are verified.
