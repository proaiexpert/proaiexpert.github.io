# PREMIUM_WEBSITE_ART_DIRECTION_MOTION_AND_EVIDENCE_PLAYBOOK_V1

> **Назначение:** перенести накопленную практическую мудрость из Financial Stream, Alina Horb, Local Repair Pro, ProAI portfolio work и независимых AI-аудитов в Website Production Factory.  
> **Применяется к:** новым client sites, niche demos, factory prototypes, service pages, case studies and portfolio integrations.  
> **Статус:** reusable factory knowledge. Client-specific facts, claims, imagery and evidence remain non-reusable.  
> **Главный принцип:** premium website — это доказательная, визуально точная и технически спокойная система, а не набор эффектов.

---

## 0. Инструкция новому агенту

Для новой factory-site задачи:

1. Прочитать factory `README.md`, `BLUEPRINT.md`, guardrails и этот playbook.
2. Определить project archetype, audience, trust model and conversion path.
3. Отделить reusable system rules от client-specific facts.
4. Сначала определить narrative, evidence and mobile behavior; потом visual effects.
5. Выбрать один signature visual/motion device.
6. Не использовать выдуманные reviews, metrics, licenses, guarantees, outcomes or before/after proof.
7. Не копировать art direction Financial Stream, Alina or Local Repair Pro целиком.
8. Все проекты должны быть complete without JavaScript and under reduced motion.
9. Не менять shared production code или публиковать без отдельного разрешения.

# 1. Factory premiumity doctrine

## 1.1 Что делает сайт премиальным

Премиальность строится в следующем порядке:

1. Понятная бизнес-задача.
2. Убедительная information architecture.
3. Конкретный next step.
4. Реальные или честно маркированные proof materials.
5. Индивидуальная визуальная среда.
6. Сильная типографика и whitespace.
7. Сдержанная motion choreography.
8. Mobile quality and performance.
9. Claims discipline.

Декоративные эффекты находятся в конце, а не в начале этой цепочки.

## 1.2 Quiet luxury для профессиональных услуг

Quiet luxury означает:

- меньше элементов;
- больше точности;
- заметную иерархию;
- взрослую палитру;
- контролируемый contrast;
- крупные доказательства;
- спокойные CTA;
- отсутствие маркетингового шума;
- отсутствие фальшивых trust signals.

Это не означает автоматически:

- black background;
- serif font;
- beige palette;
- huge whitespace;
- luxury-fashion composition.

Стиль должен соответствовать аудитории и услуге.

## 1.3 Premiumity test

Перед утверждением визуального направления спросить:

- Можно ли понять business and next step за 10–15 секунд?
- Видно ли, почему этому бизнесу можно доверять?
- Есть ли доказательства или честная маркировка их отсутствия?
- Кажется ли страница индивидуальной, а не template-tier?
- Сохраняется ли качество на mobile?
- Быстро ли загружается ключевой visual?
- Можно ли убрать 20% декоративных элементов без потери смысла?

Если последний ответ «да», лишнее нужно убрать.

# 2. Factory pipeline integration

Добавить premiumity/evidence gates к стандартному pipeline.

## Phase 1 — Intake

Собрать:

- business facts;
- audience;
- geography;
- services;
- client language;
- actual proof;
- missing proof;
- prohibited claims;
- preferred contact route;
- operational limitations;
- required trust signals;
- photography rights;
- language/localization scope.

## Phase 2 — Research

Исследовать:

- competitor sameness;
- category clichés;
- trust fears;
- questions before contact;
- service decision sequence;
- local terminology;
- mobile search behavior;
- compliance and safety risks.

Output должен включать не только «что делают конкуренты», но и:

> What should this site deliberately refuse to imitate?

## Phase 3 — Strategy

Определить:

- one-sentence claim;
- primary user problem;
- failure mode of a generic solution;
- trust architecture;
- content-before-contact sequence;
- primary CTA;
- secondary CTA;
- proof hierarchy;
- boundaries and disclaimers.

## Phase 4 — Narrative architecture

Собрать page roles and chapter/section order до визуального прототипа.

## Phase 5 — Visual direction

Подготовить одну утверждаемую концепцию с:

- project archetype;
- color world;
- type system;
- imagery policy;
- signature device;
- screenshot/proof strategy;
- desktop/mobile compositions;
- anti-pattern list.

## Phase 6 — Motion specification

Определить:

- what moves;
- why it moves;
- trigger;
- duration;
- easing;
- reduced-motion state;
- no-JS state;
- mobile simplification;
- performance risk.

## Phase 7 — Prototype

Сначала построить:

- hero;
- one evidence chapter/module;
- CTA;
- mobile opening;
- reduced-motion state.

Не строить всю страницу до owner approval ключевой режиссуры.

## Phase 8 — Production and QA

Добавить к существующему QA:

- evidence-source validation;
- motion simultaneity check;
- screenshot legibility;
- 320 px and landscape;
- reduced motion;
- no-JS;
- 200% zoom;
- LCP candidate review;
- claims comparison against source pack.

## Phase 9 — Knowledge extraction

После проекта зафиксировать:

- reusable structure;
- reusable motion pattern;
- niche-specific lessons;
- failed ideas;
- mobile defects found;
- evidence/claim rules;
- exact task patterns;
- what must not be copied to another niche.

# 3. Project archetype selection

## 3.1 System-heavy professional service

Examples:

- accounting;
- legal operations;
- automation;
- AI systems;
- complex consulting.

Best narrative:

`uncertainty → architecture → controlled process → evidence → next step`

Best visual devices:

- system map;
- signal path;
- evidence dossier;
- restrained grid;
- capability/status language.

## 3.2 Personal expert brand

Examples:

- psychologist;
- consultant;
- therapist-adjacent non-medical practice;
- coach;
- author/expert.

Best narrative:

`person → approach → boundaries → process → content → contact`

Best visual devices:

- portrait presence;
- editorial threshold;
- fine rules;
- personal typography;
- content excerpts;
- language parity.

## 3.3 Local service / trade

Examples:

- handyman;
- construction;
- repair;
- landscaping;
- cleaning;
- moving.

Best narrative:

`finished result → service scenarios → scope → send photos/request → service area → FAQ`

Best visual devices:

- result-led hero;
- scenario sequence;
- local material palette;
- service-area cues;
- send-photos flow;
- scope/safety modules.

## 3.4 Clinic / regulated professional service

Best narrative:

`service clarity → qualifications/proof → process → safety/boundaries → contact`

Requirements:

- stronger claims review;
- no medical implication without evidence;
- accessibility priority;
- emergency/boundary language where appropriate;
- avoid generic clinical blue unless strategically justified.

## 3.5 Boutique B2B studio

Best narrative:

`problem → method → selected work → evidence → founder/studio context → intake`

Avoid broad “we do everything” positioning.

# 4. Narrative pattern library

## Pattern A — Problem / System / Proof / Next step

Use for service pages and system-heavy sites.

## Pattern B — Person / Practice / Process / Contact

Use for personal brands.

## Pattern C — Outcome / Scenarios / Scope / Request

Use for local-service websites.

## Pattern D — Claim / Method / Cases / Limits / Intake

Use for studio and consultancy homepages.

## Pattern E — Before / Architecture after

Use only when factual. This is architectural comparison, not fabricated performance proof.

## Pattern F — Content before conversion

Use when expertise and trust require reading before contact.

Examples:

- Notes/articles;
- guides;
- FAQs;
- process pages;
- pricing approach.

# 5. Hero system

## 5.1 Hero jobs

A hero must establish:

- who the site is for;
- what system/result is offered;
- why the visitor should keep reading;
- primary next step;
- visual identity;
- one proof/status cue where appropriate.

## 5.2 Hero variants

### Result-led hero

Use for local service. Show finished outcome, not defects.

### Portrait-led hero

Use for personal expert. Portrait establishes authorship, not generic friendliness.

### System-led hero

Use for B2B/AI/accounting. Show architecture, interface proof or controlled process.

### Evidence-led hero

Use when one real result/status is the strongest trust anchor. Keep claims qualified.

### Editorial-threshold hero

Use when the page enters a distinct project or brand world.

## 5.3 Hero motion sequence

Preferred order:

1. structure/background visible;
2. H1 resolves;
3. primary visual resolves;
4. CTA/evidence appears;
5. decorative detail finishes.

Do not animate everything simultaneously.

# 6. Visual direction system

## 6.1 Color world

Define:

- shell/base;
- primary reading surface;
- secondary surface;
- primary ink;
- secondary ink;
- one restrained accent;
- focus color;
- status colors where semantically required.

Accent should not occupy the majority of the page.

## 6.2 Typography system

Define:

- display family;
- body/interface family;
- weights;
- line lengths;
- desktop/mobile scale;
- language-specific line breaks;
- source/status typography.

Do not add decorative fonts without a narrative role.

## 6.3 Whitespace

Whitespace separates meaning, not merely blocks.

Use larger pauses around:

- hero-to-first-proof transition;
- major evidence;
- owner quote;
- system map;
- final CTA.

Do not use identical section spacing everywhere.

## 6.4 Frame and card policy

Cards are allowed when they represent a real unit:

- service;
- scenario;
- evidence;
- step;
- article;
- status.

Do not put every paragraph in a card. Premium layouts often need fewer boundaries, not more.

# 7. Motion pattern library

Every project selects one signature pattern and a small number of supporting patterns.

## 7.1 Grid Assembly

Use for precision/system design.

- faint local grid;
- module alignment;
- grid becomes quiet;
- no permanent animated background.

## 7.2 Signal Path

Use for connected stages.

- local path;
- active node;
- content response;
- mobile vertical/static fallback.

## 7.3 Editorial Threshold

Use for entering a distinct brand world.

- color-field handoff;
- arch or material edge;
- controlled opening/closing;
- no generic wave divider.

## 7.4 Portrait Presence

- portrait visible from first frame or near-final;
- small mask/scale resolve;
- face never crossed by animated clip.

## 7.5 Evidence Dossier

- label;
- proof;
- source/date;
- limitation/live link.

## 7.6 Scenario Sequence

- one service scenario at a time;
- accessible static alternatives;
- no required carousel.

## 7.7 Language Pair Alignment

- matched screenshots;
- small stagger desktop;
- static stack mobile.

## 7.8 Screenshot Mask Reveal

- 420–560 ms;
- vertical/horizontal mask or restrained crossfade;
- no heavy mobile blur.

## 7.9 Layered Reveal

Use for 3–5 system layers.

- all content exists in final DOM state;
- no complex 3D peel;
- mobile normal flow.

## 7.10 Fine-rule trace

- decorative only;
- one or two lines at once;
- 280–520 ms;
- no continuous route across the whole site.

## 7.11 Local material handoff

Use for construction/home services:

- color/material field;
- finished-result image;
- subtle texture;
- avoid faux-realistic material gimmicks.

## 7.12 Progress navigation

Use for long pages only.

- slim chapter rail desktop;
- compact current-section marker mobile;
- accessible anchors;
- no forced scroll snapping.

## 7.13 Quote field

- real approved quote;
- large block;
- one reveal;
- no word animation.

## 7.14 CTA handoff

- closing thesis;
- one primary CTA;
- subordinate secondary link;
- calm confirmation state only on actual forms.

# 8. Motion technical grammar

## 8.1 Timing

| Element | Typical duration |
|---|---:|
| hover/focus micro-motion | 180–280 ms |
| label/rule | 280–420 ms |
| image/module reveal | 420–560 ms |
| headline | 560–700 ms |
| hero/major threshold | 760–920 ms |

## 8.2 Easing

Use one project/system easing family. Preferred expressive curve:

`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`

## 8.3 Simultaneity

- maximum two prominent effects simultaneously;
- motion follows reading order;
- avoid background, copy, image and badge motion at the same time.

## 8.4 Technical constraints

- animate transform and opacity;
- avoid top/left layout animation;
- avoid repeated filters;
- minimize observers/listeners;
- no WebGL dependency for ordinary sites;
- no scroll-jacking;
- no loader before content;
- no motion as required navigation.

## 8.5 Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion`:

- reveal final states immediately;
- stop non-essential loops;
- disable smooth scrolling;
- preserve focus and state behavior;
- do not leave hidden placeholders.

## 8.6 No-JS

- all content visible;
- all links functional;
- comparison states visible;
- no blank sections;
- menu uses best available fallback.

# 9. Evidence and trust architecture

## 9.1 Evidence categories

1. Business identity and live status.
2. Real photography or production screenshot.
3. Service/process architecture.
4. Dated metric snapshot.
5. Verified qualification/license where relevant.
6. Real testimonial.
7. Operational status and human oversight.
8. External live route.

## 9.2 Evidence density

A strong page normally needs:

- 3–5 key proof points;
- 1–2 strong visual proofs;
- optional real quote;
- clear limitation/source note.

More evidence is not automatically stronger if it becomes illegible or repetitive.

## 9.3 Claim safety

Never infer:

- revenue;
- conversion;
- leads;
- time savings;
- rankings;
- customer satisfaction;
- safety;
- compliance;
- guaranteed outcomes.

A metric needs:

- date;
- window;
- source;
- scope;
- limitation.

Do not label a small metric “strong”, “normal” or “successful” without benchmark evidence.

## 9.4 Demo projects

Demo/concept sites may prove:

- structure;
- art direction;
- responsive implementation;
- intake flow;
- niche adaptation;
- QA discipline.

They may not imply:

- real client relationship;
- real reviews;
- real service licenses;
- completed jobs;
- business performance.

# 10. Screenshot and imagery system

## 10.1 Source vs public assets

Maintain:

- source capture inventory;
- selected public asset manifest;
- owner approval record;
- route/language/viewport/date metadata.

## 10.2 Screenshot rules

- focused crop;
- legible hierarchy;
- no full-page miniature;
- no personal data;
- no console/errors/tokens;
- no fake browser/device unless evidence requires it;
- explicit dimensions;
- responsive formats;
- lazy load below fold;
- natural mobile ratio.

## 10.3 Photography rules

- use real client photography only with rights;
- use scenario imagery when proof is unavailable, clearly not as completed-job evidence;
- hero should show desired outcome or identity, not fear/defect imagery;
- avoid generic sentimental stock clichés;
- preserve face/hands and subject integrity.

# 11. CTA and intake system

## 11.1 CTA hierarchy

- one primary action;
- one secondary action;
- optional tertiary text link.

## 11.2 CTA copy

Name the next step:

- Request an estimate.
- Send photos.
- Discuss your project.
- Start with project context.
- View the live site.

Avoid vague `Learn more` when a concrete next step exists.

## 11.3 Context before calendar

For complex professional services:

1. collect context;
2. confirm fit/scope;
3. route to meeting where useful.

Do not force calendar booking before the visitor understands the process.

## 11.4 Form design

- ask only what is needed;
- separate required/optional fields;
- explain sensitive-data limits;
- localize validation/success;
- include privacy consent where required;
- use anti-spam layers without absolute security claims;
- success state explains what happens next.

## 11.5 CTA responsive behavior

- portrait stacked;
- landscape two columns only when labels fit;
- tablet/desktop bounded row;
- equal practical height;
- no two uncontrolled full-width strips;
- external links clearly marked.

# 12. Mobile premiumity

## 12.1 Mobile is a separate composition

Reconsider:

- order;
- crop;
- density;
- CTA placement;
- motion;
- sticky behavior;
- menu height;
- line breaks.

## 12.2 Required mobile checks

- 320×700;
- 360×800;
- 390×844;
- 430×932;
- representative landscape widths;
- tablet portrait/landscape;
- 200% zoom where possible.

## 12.3 Mobile rules

- no horizontal overflow;
- no fixed CTA covering content;
- no forced 100vh in short landscape;
- one or two animations at once;
- remove blur-heavy effects;
- tappable controls ≥44 px practical size;
- drawer scrolls internally;
- screenshots remain legible;
- full-width modules stay inside safe margins.

# 13. Performance as premiumity

## 13.1 Core requirements

- identify one LCP asset;
- preload only that asset when justified;
- responsive images;
- explicit dimensions/aspect ratios;
- below-fold lazy loading;
- minimal route-specific JS;
- no effect-only third-party scripts;
- stable font fallback;
- avoid layout thrash;
- transform/opacity motion.

## 13.2 Image formats

Use WebP/AVIF where production support and tooling are reliable. Preserve high-quality masters outside the delivery path.

## 13.3 Performance review

Lab targets are acceptance targets, not public claims. Field claims require dated CrUX/Search Console evidence.

# 16. Common anti-patterns

Do not:

- build every niche from the same visual template;
- use permanent animated grids;
- animate every paragraph;
- rely on blur/glow for premiumity;
- create fake dashboards;
- add filters/cards before content justifies them;
- use testimonials as placeholders;
- publish unverified metrics;
- overuse serif, beige or black as universal luxury codes;
- hide important information behind hover;
- use horizontal galleries as sole mobile access;
- hotlink client assets;
- place working duplicate forms on case-study pages;
- add effects before owner approves composition;
- push directly to production.

# 17. Premiumity QA checklist

## Strategy

- one clear audience;
- one primary problem;
- one primary CTA;
- trust fears addressed;
- claims sourced.

## Narrative

- problem before service list;
- each section has one job;
- evidence follows claims;
- no repeated thesis;
- next step clear.

## Visual

- one concept;
- one signature device;
- distinct from prior projects;
- readable copy width;
- evidence large enough;
- whitespace varied intentionally.

## Motion

- maximum two prominent simultaneous effects;
- timings specified;
- reduced motion complete;
- no-JS complete;
- no heavy mobile filters;
- no scroll-jacking.

## Mobile

- 320 px pass;
- landscape pass;
- no overflow;
- no clipped subject;
- CTA consistent;
- menu and footer usable.

## Accessibility

- semantic headings;
- visible focus;
- contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- alt text;
- 200% zoom;
- no screenshot-only essential information.

## Performance

- LCP asset controlled;
- image sizes explicit;
- lazy loading correct;
- JS budget reviewed;
- no unnecessary library;
- console clean.

## Evidence

- source/date/scope recorded;
- permission recorded;
- no fake outcomes;
- demo status clear;
- privacy/test data absent.

# 19. Final control statement

The Website Production Factory must accumulate systems, not clone pages. Financial Stream contributes evidence discipline and system storytelling. Alina contributes human-scale editorial identity and boundary-led trust. Local Repair Pro contributes result-led local-service structure and mobile request clarity. Future sites should combine the right principles for their niche without visually imitating any one reference project.
