A service business can be discovered in four places that look similar to a buyer but work differently behind the scenes: ordinary Google results, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, ChatGPT search, and Google Maps/local results. The practical question is not “How do we get AI to recommend us?” It is which discovery path matters to our customers, what evidence is missing, and which investment will help a qualified person choose us?

For the underlying website foundation—service clarity, trust, crawlability, conversion, and operations—see our companion AI-ready website decision guide. This article addresses a narrower, subsequent decision: where visibility work should go, how to test eligibility, and how to measure useful discovery without inventing an “AI visibility score.”

Search channels are different

One customer may type “emergency drain repair near me”; another may ask for an explanation of repair options; a third may request a comparison of local providers. The same webpage can help across those journeys, but the surfaces, inclusion rules, and measurement methods must not be collapsed.

AI Overviews and AI Mode are not identical interfaces. An AI Overview is a generated summary shown within eligible Google Search results when Google considers it useful. AI Mode is an interactive AI-powered search experience for deeper exploration, nuanced comparisons and follow-up questions. Both may include links to supporting websites, but neither a summary nor a source link is guaranteed. Google does not prescribe separate ranking algorithms or special optimization requirements for these two experiences. Google's official explanation.

Eligibility before visibility

Before paying for new content, test whether existing public pages can actually be found and read. For Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Google's stated baseline is an indexed page eligible for a Search snippet; there is no separate mandatory AI-specific markup. Indexability is eligibility, not proof of exposure. Google also documents that its generative answers may search related subquestions, so a specific, useful page can address a smaller part of a larger decision. Google Search Central.

For ChatGPT search, OpenAI distinguishes OAI-SearchBot from GPTBot: the first is used for search discovery; the second relates to model training. A publisher may allow search access without making the same decision about training. ChatGPT-User is a separate, user-initiated agent and is not the search opt-out control. Check both robots.txt and firewall/CDN handling of published crawler addresses. This is a technical access check, not a citation purchase.

  1. Inspect the exact URL. Confirm it returns useful HTML without authentication, a CAPTCHA, accidental noindex, misleading canonical, or blocked essential content.
  2. Check Google Search Console. Inspect index status and actual search queries. A sitemap is a discovery aid, not a promise of indexing.
  3. Check the Google generative-search setting. In Google Search Console, open Settings → Search generative AI for the verified property. Inclusion in eligible generative AI features is the default unless excluded; a child property may inherit a parent property's setting, so inspect both effective and inherited choices. This is a distinct inclusion control—not a substitute for Googlebot access, indexing or snippet eligibility, and not a ranking tactic. Being included does not guarantee an appearance. Google's control instructions.
  4. Review OpenAI access deliberately. Distinguish OAI-SearchBot permissions from GPTBot and validate server/WAF behavior; respect the publisher's choices.
  5. Read the mobile page without scripts. A service, geographic boundary, trust evidence and contact path should not disappear if interaction scripts fail.
  6. Use structured data truthfully. Existing Article and Breadcrumb JSON-LD may describe visible facts; adding unrelated markup is not a way to force AI citations.

Stop condition: If the primary service page is blocked or cannot clearly state what the company does and where it works, do not spend the next dollar on “AI citations.” Repair the underlying access and decision information first.

The evidence-to-claim matrix

A claim is useful when a buyer can check its scope. “Trusted experts” is promotional language; “licensed for these types of work in this state,” if documented and current, is a verifiable fact. Publish credentials only with valid supporting records. Avoid publishing private client information or presenting a staged case as a customer result.

Evidence is not a decorative trust badge. It is an operating commitment: if claims cannot be maintained, narrow them. The purpose is to make a correct decision possible, whether the visitor arrived from a traditional result, a generated answer, a map listing, or a referral.

Pages that help a buyer decide

Start with one strong page per genuinely distinct service intent—not dozens of interchangeable city-and-keyword pages. Each page should answer: what you do, who qualifies, where you can operate, what is included and excluded, how work begins, what determines cost, and what can be independently verified. Then connect pages with meaningful internal links: service → proof → process → contact.

Use headings to organise actual decisions, not fragments written to resemble AI prompts. A concise direct answer helps the customer; repetitive AI-targeted “question chunks” do not create special Google eligibility. Google explicitly discourages synthetic pages made for query variants and says no special llms.txt file or AI-specific structured data is required for AI Search. Official Google guidance.

For companies working in more than one language, a second locale is an operational promise: service details, directions, intake replies, and policies must be maintainable in that language. Use the multilingual website decision model to decide whether focused translations or full coverage are justified. The technical architecture should support useful canonical and hreflang signals rather than publishing thin translations solely to capture extra queries.

Local search has separate signals

When purchase depends on proximity, Google Maps and local results may be more directly actionable than a broad informational article. According to Google Business Profile guidance, local ranking is mainly influenced by relevance, distance and prominence. A contractor cannot buy a guaranteed position and cannot change how far a customer is from its service area.

Verify the actual category, phone, working hours, service area and public address rules. Explain individual services accurately on both your website and Business Profile. Encourage authentic reviews without inventing customer stories; respond appropriately and protect personal details. Don't manufacture location pages for places you do not serve. A locality named in a heading is not proof of local operational coverage.

Measure the handoff, not only the mention

A screenshot showing an AI answer mentioning a brand is an observation at a moment, not a stable ranking or a confirmed business outcome. Record the query, location, date, interface and linked URL. Repeated manual tests can help spot omissions, but they cannot prove comprehensive share of visibility or identify the algorithm behind a response.

Google Search Console's official Generative AI performance report (Search) tracks impressions from supported Google Search generative AI experiences, including AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google announced that the worldwide website rollout was complete on August 31, 2026; the report may still be hidden when a property has too few eligible impressions. Its documented breakdowns include pages, countries, dates and devices. These are impression measures, not separate qualified-lead, conversion, or feature-specific click attribution reports. Use the conventional Web Performance report for overall Search activity, website Analytics for visits and contact actions, Google Business Profile for local interactions, and actual enquiry records to judge commercial value. Do not infer a lead from an AI impression. Google's report documentation.

A practical measurement chain: query or discovery source → landing page → service-fit action → valid enquiry → accepted project conversation. Use UTM/referrer data where available; do not label unattributed direct visits as “ChatGPT leads.” Report unknown attribution explicitly. Separate lead volume from lead quality, and never claim uplift without pre/post data and a defensible comparison.

Worked example: a local service business

Illustrative scenario only — not a client, campaign or measured result. Assume a fictional residential HVAC business operates in two neighbouring counties, handles scheduled installation and maintenance, and has a four-person team. Its website says “complete comfort solutions,” shows a stock image, mentions five cities, and sends every enquiry into one general mailbox. There are no real baseline traffic figures in this example.

Diagnosis: The first problem is not whether ChatGPT cites the company. Customers cannot see whether it performs their job, how far it travels, what influences a quotation, or when to expect a response. Some claimed service cities may also be incorrect. This creates risk in all channels and can waste paid attention.

  1. Week one: verify location coverage, Google Business Profile and Search Console access. Ask the dispatcher which service enquiries are repeatedly unsuitable.
  2. Weeks two to four: produce a clearly scoped maintenance page, an installation page with selection and cost factors, and a genuine step-by-step contact explanation. State what the company does not offer.
  3. Month two: add one permissioned real project explanation only if truthful and documentable. Fix forms, ownership and follow-up. No synthetic rating or invented client quote.
  4. Month three: compare actual qualified enquiries by page and source; adjust the confusing sections, not just their keywords.

Decision rule: If the company cannot verify its scope, operations and response path, allocate the next unit of budget to those facts and the intake workflow. If those are strong and the site is accessible, test the content questions that prospects actually ask. AI-specific experiments come last.

What to fund next

Choose the next investment by the first verified bottleneck: access → accurate business facts → service-level evidence → a workable contact path → measurement → measured refinement. If a company already has strong content and enquiries but underperforms on a specific channel, analyse that channel separately. Do not buy bundled “SEO + AI ranking” promises without a testable scope and ownership model.
